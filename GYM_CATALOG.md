### 🎗️ Resistance bands

TWO KINDS, both on hand. (1) LOOP BANDS — a 4-band set of flat continuous loops, 208 cm round, 3 mm thick, colour-coded by strength: YELLOW 0.64 cm (20 lb label), RED 1.3 cm (35 lb), BLACK 2.1 cm (65 lb), PURPLE 3.2 cm (85 lb). The labels are the vendor MAXIMUM and these are 3 mm rather than the usual 4.5 mm, so every band runs LIGHTER than its number — treat the colour as the unit, not the pounds. ALWAYS name the colour when prescribing a band exercise; the bands themselves are not printed with any weight. (2) OLDER TUBE BANDS with handles and a door anchor. Both can loop around the basement post, so the anchor can sit at ANY height. DELIBERATELY NARROW ROLE, confirmed by the owner: dumbbells are ALWAYS preferred when they can do the job, so bands are NOT used for pressing, rowing, pulldowns, curls, pushdowns or wrist work — the dumbbells and the pull-up bar cover all of those. Bands are kept for exactly two things they alone can do: (a) light prehab BELOW the 8.5 lb dumbbell floor (rotator-cuff external rotation, scaption); (b) HORIZONTAL resistance, which gravity cannot supply (Pallof press, rotational press), plus the two knee-isolation moves the fixed-roller bench cannot do. Never prescribe a band exercise that has a dumbbell equivalent. Progress by moving further from the anchor first, then by changing colour. ONE MORE JOB, added with the Nordic pad: a band can ASSIST a movement instead of resisting it (looped over the pull-up bar and under the chest to take weight off a Nordic curl). That is not covered by the "never where a dumbbell would do" rule above — nothing else here can unload a bodyweight eccentric — and it progresses BACKWARDS: thickest band first, lighter as you get stronger.

### 〰️ Flexible resistance bar

FitBeast flexible resistance bar — a solid rubber bar you BEND and TWIST (the TheraBand FlexBar pattern), not a band and not a weight. THREE bars, colour-coded: RED = 10 lb, GREEN = 15 lb, BLUE = 25 lb. The number is roughly the force needed to bend the bar into a full C, not a load you lift, and the bars carry no printed figure — ALWAYS name the COLOUR when prescribing, the way band colour is named. DELIBERATELY NARROW ROLE, same rule as the bands: dumbbells win wherever they can do the job, so this is NOT a curl, a press or a general forearm tool — wrist curls, reverse wrist curls and every carry stay on the dumbbells and the kettlebell. It is kept for the three things nothing else in the basement can do: (a) ECCENTRIC wrist-extensor loading (Tyler twist) and wrist-flexor loading (reverse Tyler twist) — the standard, well-evidenced work for tennis and golfer's elbow, which is the racquet-sport injury this gym has no other answer to; (b) resisted forearm SUPINATION / PRONATION, the rotation a pickleball stroke actually does and a dumbbell cannot load through range; (c) all of it seated, in no floor space, with no anchor. Start every twist on RED and only move to GREEN when red is easy through the full range with a slow 4-second release; BLUE is a grip bar, not a starting point. Ninety seconds of this is prehab, not a workout — never the main movement of a session.

### 🦵 Nordic curl pad

A portable Nordic hamstring curl device — a padded knee board with a foot roller that pins the ankles. Sets up on the floor in seconds, no rack, no partner holding your heels. It does exactly ONE movement and there is no substitute for it in this basement: a knee-flexion ECCENTRIC with the hips locked, which is the single best-evidenced hamstring injury-prevention exercise there is, and it loads the hamstrings HARD with ZERO spinal load — the reason it matters here, where the other two hamstring options (Romanian deadlift, kettlebell swing) are both flagged backRisk. OWNER STATUS, confirmed: an unassisted Nordic is currently IMPOSSIBLE — he cannot control the descent at all. That is normal and it is not a reason to skip the movement; it is a reason to prescribe an ASSISTED version. The ladder is band-assisted → hands-catch negatives → unassisted, and it is walked in that order over months, never rushed. Never prescribe the unassisted Nordic until the assisted one is easy for 3 × 6 with a slow descent. Hips STAY EXTENDED throughout on every version — the moment the movement becomes a hip hinge it stops being a hamstring exercise and starts being a lower back one.

*Used by 3 exercises.*
